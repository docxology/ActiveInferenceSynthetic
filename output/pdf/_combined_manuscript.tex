\section{Abstract}\label{sec:abstract}

Active Inference casts perception and action as free-energy
minimization. This overview shows how Bayesian mechanics carries the
Free Energy Principle from equilibrium to far-from-equilibrium life.

\newpage

\section{Extended Abstract}\label{sec:extended}

A thing that persists must, in effect, hold a model of the world that
keeps it intact. \textbf{Active Inference} turns that observation into
mechanics: a system maintains itself by minimizing \emph{variational
free energy}, a single quantity that scores how badly its internal
expectations fit incoming sensations
\citep{friston2010fep, parr2022active}. Perception lowers free energy by
updating beliefs; action lowers it by changing the world so the beliefs
come true. One currency, two directions --- inference and control ---
which is why the same template reappears from single cells to brains to
institutions.

The bound is what makes this \emph{applicable} rather than metaphorical.
Variational free energy is an upper bound on \textbf{surprise}, the
negative log evidence \(-\ln p(s)\) a system assigns to its own sensory
states. Because the bound is computable from a system's own beliefs,
minimizing it \emph{is} approximate Bayesian inference carried out by
the physical dynamics of the thing itself. The accounting is organized
by a \textbf{Markov blanket}: a statistical boundary splitting
\emph{external} states \(\eta\) from \emph{internal} states \(\mu\),
coupled only through \emph{sensory} and \emph{active} blanket states.
Internal states come to parameterize beliefs about the external world
they can never touch directly --- the core claim of \textbf{Bayesian
mechanics}, a physics written in terms of, and by, those beliefs
\citep{dacosta2021bayesian, ramstead2023bayesian}.

fig.~\ref{fig:overview} shows the move that this overview is built
around, without a single equation of derivation. Both panels live on
\emph{the same} free-energy landscape --- a Gaussian bowl whose floor is
the system's expected state. At \textbf{equilibrium} (panel a) the flow
is pure descent, \(f=-\nabla F\): every trajectory falls straight to the
mode and stops. Detailed balance holds, there is no net current, and ---
taken literally --- nothing further happens. A rock is at equilibrium;
so, eventually, is a system that has stopped doing anything. Living
systems occupy panel (b). There the flow gains a \emph{solenoidal} term,
\(f=(Q-\Gamma)\nabla F\), that runs along the contours of \(F\) rather
than down them. Free energy is still minimized --- the system still
tracks its niche --- yet probability mass \textbf{circulates}
indefinitely in a \textbf{non-equilibrium steady state}. That persistent
current is the signature of things that stay far from equilibrium:
heartbeats, foraging, perception that never settles. The gradient part
is the Free Energy Principle's ``climbing down surprise''; the
solenoidal part is what keeps the system alive while doing so
\citep{friston2023bayesian}.

Read the two panels together and the headline of Active Inference is
visible at a glance: the \emph{same} imperative --- minimize free energy
--- produces inert relaxation in one regime and open-ended,
self-sustaining behavior in the other. The rest of this overview unpacks
that picture in the spirit of \emph{show, not tell}: each section adds
one runnable, minimal demonstration rather than another layer of
formalism. sec.~\ref{sec:generative-model} makes the \emph{inference}
concrete --- that minimizing variational free energy over a generative
model \textbf{is} Bayesian updating, and that free energy is a tight
upper bound on surprise. sec.~\ref{sec:worked-regimes} makes the
\emph{mechanics} concrete --- integrating the flow above into worked
equilibrium and non-equilibrium trajectories and quantifying the
persistent current that separates the living regime from the inert one.
sec.~\ref{sec:expected-free-energy} turns to \emph{action} ---
minimizing the same currency over policies, whose pragmatic and
epistemic terms produce the explore/exploit balance from one rule.
sec.~\ref{sec:markov-blanket} exhibits the \emph{boundary} itself ---
the Markov blanket that makes internal states inferring an external
world a well-posed idea rather than a metaphor.
sec.~\ref{sec:steady-state} quantifies the far-from-equilibrium regime
outright --- the analytic probability current and the entropy-production
rate that is zero exactly at detailed balance and positive out of it.
Finally sec.~\ref{sec:formal} closes the loop on rigour: the
load-bearing theorems --- Gibbs' inequality, the free-energy bound, the
conservative solenoidal current, the sign of entropy production --- are
\textbf{machine-checked in Lean 4}, proved from the kernel's axioms with
no \texttt{sorry} and no external library.

\section{Perception as Inference}\label{sec:generative-model}

The extended abstract makes a strong claim in one sentence:
\emph{minimizing variational free energy \textbf{is} approximate
Bayesian inference, carried out by the physical dynamics of the thing
itself}. This section makes that claim runnable. It is the smallest
generative model that still says something true, and everything below is
checked against closed-form Bayes in \breaktt{src/generative\_model.py}.

A system holds a \textbf{generative model} of a hidden cause \(x\) and a
sensation \(s\): a prior \(p(x)\) over states and a likelihood
\(p(s \mid x)\) saying which sensations each state tends to produce.
Together they fix the joint \(p(s, x) = p(s \mid x)\,p(x)\), the
evidence \(p(s) = \sum_x p(s, x)\), and --- by Bayes' rule --- the
posterior \(p(x \mid s) = p(s, x) / p(s)\). The system never sees \(x\);
it only ever has \(s\) and its own model. Inference is the problem of
recovering \(p(x \mid s)\) from those.

Active Inference solves it without ever computing \(p(s)\) directly,
through a belief \(q(x)\) and a single scalar, the \textbf{variational
free energy}:

\[
F[q] \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{KL}\!\left[q(x)\,\Vert\,p(x)\right]}_{\text{complexity}}
\;-\; \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q}\!\left[\ln p(s \mid x)\right]}_{\text{accuracy}}.
\]

Two facts, both asserted to machine precision in the tests, make \(F\)
the whole game. First, \textbf{\(F\) is an upper bound on surprise}:
\(F[q] = -\ln p(s) +
\mathrm{KL}\!\left[q \Vert p(x \mid s)\right] \ge -\ln p(s)\), because a
KL divergence is never negative. Surprise \(-\ln p(s)\) is exactly what
an organism ``wants'' to keep low --- the improbability of its own
sensory state --- but it cannot compute it. It \emph{can} compute \(F\),
from quantities it already has. Second, \textbf{the bound is tight at
the posterior}: \(F\) is least, and equals the surprise, precisely when
\(q = p(x \mid s)\). So the belief that minimizes free energy is the
Bayesian posterior, and the minimized value is the surprise it bounds.
Inference falls out of minimization.

fig.~\ref{fig:belief} shows this happening. Take a two-state model with
prior \(p(x) = (0.60, 0.40)\) and a likelihood under which state \(x_0\)
makes the observed sensation \(s\) far more probable than \(x_1\) does.
Panel (a) starts from a maximally uncommitted uniform belief and runs
gradient descent on \(F\) over the belief simplex; the belief mass
migrates toward the exact posterior
\(p(x_0 \mid s) = 0.54/0.62 \approx 0.87\). Panel (b) tracks \(F\)
across the same descent: it falls \textbf{monotonically} to the surprise
\(-\ln p(s)\) (dashed line), and the shrinking shaded gap is exactly
\(\mathrm{KL}\!\left[q_t \Vert p(x \mid
s)\right]\) --- the belief's remaining error. Perception, here, is
nothing but this descent: the system lowers free energy by updating
beliefs, and in doing so recovers the posterior it could not compute
directly.

The complexity--accuracy split names the trade-off. \emph{Accuracy}
rewards beliefs that explain the sensation; \emph{complexity} penalizes
moving away from the prior. Perception moves beliefs only as far as the
evidence warrants --- the same regularized fit that makes the
free-energy principle a principle rather than a slogan. Action, treated
in the next demonstrations, minimizes the \emph{same} \(F\) in the other
direction: by changing \(s\) rather than \(q\).

\section{Worked Regimes: Equilibrium and
Non-Equilibrium}\label{sec:worked-regimes}

The overview figure (fig.~\ref{fig:overview}) contrasts two \emph{flows}
--- one that comes to rest, one that circulates --- as arrow fields. A
flow field says where a system would go next; it does not, on its own,
show where a system \emph{ends up}. This section integrates the flow
into actual \textbf{trajectories}, so the equilibrium\texorpdfstring{\ensuremath{\leftrightarrow}}{<->}non-equilibrium
distinction becomes a path you can watch, and the ``persistent current''
of living systems becomes a number rather than an adjective. The
integrator and its diagnostics live in \breaktt{src/trajectories.py},
built directly on the tested flow of \breaktt{src/free\_energy\_flow.py}.

Both regimes obey the Bayesian-mechanics flow
\(f = (Q - \Gamma)\nabla F\) on the \emph{same} isotropic Gaussian
free-energy landscape. The only difference is the antisymmetric
solenoidal operator \(Q\): zero at equilibrium, nonzero in a
non-equilibrium steady state (NESS). A fixed-step fourth-order
Runge--Kutta scheme (no adaptive stepping, no randomness) advances a
starting point under this flow. That the integrator can be trusted is
not assumed: at equilibrium the flow reduces to the linear system
\(\dot{x} = -\gamma\,\Pi\,x\) (with \(\Pi\) the precision), and the
tests confirm the RK4 path matches the closed form
\(x(t) = x_0\,e^{-\gamma\,\Pi\,t}\) --- i.e.~\(x_0 e^{-\gamma t}\) at
unit precision --- to better than \(10^{-6}\).

fig.~\ref{fig:trajectories} shows the two fates. In panel (a), the
\textbf{equilibrium} trajectory (\(Q = 0\)) falls straight down the
free-energy gradient to the mode and stops. Free energy decreases
monotonically along the path, the radius collapses to under 1\% of its
starting value, and the net angle swept about the mode is zero to
numerical precision --- detailed balance, no current. This is a rock
settling, a battery draining, a belief that has finished inferring.

Panel (b) is the \textbf{NESS} (\(Q \neq 0\)). The dissipative term
still pulls the system down \(F\) --- free energy again decreases
monotonically, so the system is \emph{still} minimizing surprise --- yet
the solenoidal term drives it \emph{around} the level sets of \(F\) at
the same time. The result is a spiral that sweeps many full turns before
relaxing inward.

How much it winds is worth stating carefully. The \emph{total} net
rotation --- the figure's \(\approx -4.58\) turns over the demonstration
window \(T=12\) --- is not an intrinsic property of the NESS; it grows
with \(T\) and would diverge as \(T \to \infty\). The intrinsic,
initial-condition-free quantity is the \textbf{rotation rate}. For this
isotropic-Gaussian flow the angular velocity is analytically constant,
\(\dot\theta = -\omega\,\Pi\) radians per unit time (with \(\Pi\) the
precision), so the rate is \(\approx -0.38\) turns per unit time
(\(-\omega\,\Pi/(2\pi)\) at \(\omega=2.4\), \(\Pi=1\)) for \emph{any}
starting point off the mode --- and it is exactly \(0\) at equilibrium
for every window. That zero-versus-nonzero rate is the honest,
quantitative witness that detailed balance is broken. The tests pin it
directly: net rotation equals \(-\omega\,\Pi\,T\) across several
starting points to a tenth of a percent.

One more distinction keeps the claim precise. Taken on its own, the
deterministic trajectory here \emph{relaxes} --- it spirals into the
mode and would sit there. A genuine non-equilibrium steady state is a
property of the \emph{stochastic} system: a stationary density
\(\rho \propto e^{-F}\) carrying a divergence-free probability current
\(j = Q\nabla F\,\rho\) that never dies. What the figure shows is that
system's \textbf{deterministic drift}, and the spiral is the mechanistic
\emph{origin} of that current, not a measurement of it. So the honest
reading of ``NESS'' here is precise: the nonzero winding
\textbf{witnesses} the solenoidal \(Q \neq 0\) that breaks detailed
balance and thereby renders the corresponding stochastic stationary
state a non-equilibrium one. Reverse the sign of \(Q\) and the
circulation reverses with it, as the tests confirm.
(sec.~\ref{sec:steady-state} supplies the measurement this section
withholds --- the actual probability current and its entropy-production
rate.)

Read the two panels against the abstract's thesis. Descent --- the Free
Energy Principle's ``climbing down surprise'' --- is common to both; it
is present in the falling free energy of \emph{each} trajectory. What
separates the living regime from the inert one is not whether free
energy is minimized but whether a conservative current runs
\emph{alongside} the descent, keeping the system in perpetual, ordered
motion while it stays close to its expected states. Equilibrium is where
things stop; the non-equilibrium steady state is where things stay
alive.

\section{Action: Variational vs Expected Free
Energy}\label{sec:expected-free-energy}

sec.~\ref{sec:generative-model} showed perception: minimizing
\textbf{variational} free energy \(F\) over beliefs about what is
happening \emph{now}. But a thing that only perceived would be a
spectator. Active Inference closes the loop by minimizing the
\emph{same} currency in the other direction --- over \textbf{actions},
scored by the free energy they are \emph{expected} to produce. That
forward-looking quantity is \textbf{expected free energy} \(G\), and it
is what turns inference into behavior. The core is
\breaktt{src/expected\_free\_energy.py}; every value below is checked
against a hand-worked reference.

The two free energies differ only in tense. \(F[q]\) grades a belief
against a sensation already in hand --- it is retrospective, and its
minimum is the posterior. \(G(a)\) grades a \emph{candidate action}
against the sensations it \emph{would} generate, averaged over what the
agent currently believes. For an action \(a\), belief \(q(s)\), outcome
model \(p(o \mid s, a)\), and preferences \(p_{\text{pref}}(o)\) --- a
distribution over the outcomes the agent wants to encounter --- \(G\)
splits into two terms the agent seeks to make large (so it minimizes
their negatives):

\[
G(a) \;=\; -\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q(o\mid a)}\big[\ln p_{\text{pref}}(o)\big]}_{\text{pragmatic value}}
\;-\; \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q(o\mid a)}\!\big[\mathrm{KL}[\,q(s\mid o,a)\,\Vert\,q(s)\,]\big]}_{\text{epistemic value}}.
\]

\textbf{Pragmatic value} is expected utility: how well an action's
likely outcomes match what the agent prefers. \textbf{Epistemic value}
is expected information gain --- how much the action's outcomes would
sharpen the belief about the hidden state, which is exactly the mutual
information \(I(s; o \mid a)\) and is always non-negative. One is the
drive to \emph{get what you want}; the other is the drive to \emph{find
out}. Both live inside a single number.

fig.~\ref{fig:efe} shows why that unification matters. An agent is
unsure which of two contexts holds (\(q(s) = (0.50, 0.50)\)) and can
take one of two actions: a \textbf{cue} that would reveal the context
(highly informative, epistemic value \(+0.37\) nats, but
preference-neutral) or a \textbf{bet} that pays off regardless of
context (uninformative, epistemic value \(0\), but potentially
rewarding). The \emph{only} thing that changes between the two panels is
the preferences. Under \textbf{flat} preferences (panel a) the agent has
nothing to chase, so the epistemic term decides: it takes the cue and
\textbf{explores} (\(G = 0.33\) vs 0.69). Under \textbf{sharp}
preferences (panel b) the pull of the reward dominates, and the same
minimization takes the bet and \textbf{exploits} (\(G = 0.43\) vs 0.84).
This is a deliberately constructed two-action example, chosen to isolate
the mechanism; what it demonstrates is structural, not incidental to
these numbers. The explore/exploit trade-off is not a separate rule
bolted onto the agent --- there is one rule, \(\arg\min_a G\). Because
\(G\) carries both a pragmatic and an epistemic term, exploration and
exploitation are the two limits of a single balance, and which one wins
is decided by the preferences alone.

This is the whole architecture in miniature. Perception (\(F\)) and
action (\(G\)) are one imperative --- minimize free energy --- read in
two tenses. Inference makes beliefs fit the world; action makes the
world fit beliefs \emph{and} preferences, gathering information where it
is uncertain and reward where it is confident.

\section{The Markov Blanket}\label{sec:markov-blanket}

Everything so far assumed a clean split between a system and its world
--- a belief \(q(s)\) about \emph{hidden} states, sensations \(o\) that
are not those states, actions that reach \emph{out}. That split is not a
modeling convenience; it is a structural claim, and it has a name: the
\textbf{Markov blanket}. This section shows what the blanket actually
asserts, using the one case where it is exactly computable --- a jointly
Gaussian world. The core is \breaktt{src/markov\_blanket.py}.

Partition the variables of a system-plus-environment into four roles:
\emph{external} states \(\eta\) (the world), \emph{internal} states
\(\mu\) (the system's own states, which parameterize its beliefs), and
the \emph{blanket} that lies between them --- \emph{sensory} states
\(s\) (world \(\to\) system) and \emph{active} states \(a\) (system
\(\to\) world). The blanket's defining property is a \textbf{conditional
independence}:

\[
p(\eta, \mu \mid s, a) \;=\; p(\eta \mid s, a)\; p(\mu \mid s, a).
\]

Internal and external states are \emph{not} independent --- they are
statistically coupled, that is the entire point of a mind modeling a
world. But that coupling is \textbf{screened by} the blanket. Fix the
blanket, and they become conditionally independent: nothing about
\(\eta\) tells you anything more about \(\mu\) once you know \(s\) and
\(a\). (This is a statement about statistical dependence in the joint
distribution, not about causal direction or intervention --- a causal
reading would need a dynamical or interventional model on top of this
one.) That conditional independence is what licenses treating internal
states as \emph{inferring} external ones they never share a variable
with: conditioned on the blanket, the internal states' sufficient
statistics for the external world are exactly their beliefs given the
blanket.

fig.~\ref{fig:blanket} makes both halves of the claim visible on a
Gaussian built so that the \(\eta\)--\(\mu\) block of its
\textbf{precision matrix} (the inverse covariance) is exactly zero.
Panel (a) is the ordinary correlation matrix: \(\eta\) and \(\mu\) are
plainly correlated (\(\mathrm{corr} = 0.19\)): they are statistically
coupled through the blanket. Panel (b) is the precision matrix, and the
payoff is the highlighted cell: \(\Pi_{\eta\mu} = 0\). In a Gaussian a
zero precision entry is \emph{exactly} a conditional independence given
the remaining variables --- so the zero at \((\eta,\mu)\) says
\(\eta \perp \mu \mid \{s,a\}\). Conditioning on the blanket collapses
the partial correlation between internal and external states to zero
(the tests confirm it vanishes to \(10^{-10}\) via the Schur
complement), even though conditioning on only \emph{part} of the blanket
does not --- both the sensory and active states are needed to close the
channel.

That is the geometry underneath the whole overview. A
free-energy-minimizing system is one whose internal states, across its
blanket, come to encode a posterior over the external states it is
coupled to but cannot see. Perception (sec.~\ref{sec:generative-model})
updates that encoding; action (sec.~\ref{sec:expected-free-energy}) acts
through the blanket to keep the world within the range the encoding
expects; and the flow and trajectories
(secs.~\ref{sec:extended}, \ref{sec:worked-regimes}) are what that
coupling looks like as dynamics. The blanket is the boundary that makes
``a thing that models its world'' a well-posed idea rather than a
metaphor.

\section{The Steady State, Quantified}\label{sec:steady-state}

sec.~\ref{sec:worked-regimes} was deliberately careful about one thing.
The deterministic spiral it drew is the \emph{drift} of a
non-equilibrium steady state, not the state itself --- a single
trajectory relaxes to the mode and stops, while a genuine steady state
is a property of a whole \emph{stochastic} ensemble, with a current that
never dies. This closing section supplies that missing object, still
analytically and with no randomness, in \breaktt{src/steady\_state.py}.
It is where ``far from equilibrium'' stops being a picture and becomes
two numbers: a \textbf{probability current} and an
\textbf{entropy-production rate}.

Give the Bayesian-mechanics flow a matched noise term --- the
Ornstein--Uhlenbeck diffusion \(dx = -Mx\,dt + \sqrt{2D}\,dW\) with
drift \(M = (\Gamma - Q)P\) and, by the fluctuation--dissipation
relation, diffusion \(D = \Gamma\). Three exact results follow, each
checked against a closed form in the tests.

First, the ensemble settles into a \textbf{stationary density}. Its
covariance solves the Lyapunov equation
\(M\Sigma + \Sigma M^{\mathsf T} = 2D\), and with \(D=\Gamma\) the
solution is exactly \(\Sigma = P^{-1}\) --- the system's own belief
covariance. The distribution the noisy system lives in is precisely
\(\rho \propto e^{-F}\): free-energy minimization and the steady state
are the same fixed point.

Second, that density carries a \textbf{probability current}
\(j(x) = b(x)\rho - D\nabla\rho = (QPx)\,\rho(x)\). When \(Q=0\) the
current is zero \emph{everywhere} (detailed balance --- the density just
sits), and when \(Q\neq0\) it is a divergence-free field that circulates
around the mode without end. This is the sustained current the
deterministic trajectory could only gesture at; here it is an explicit
vector field, orthogonal to \(\nabla F\) (the tests confirm both
\(\nabla\!\cdot j = 0\) and \(j\perp\nabla F\) to machine precision).

Third, the current has a price. The \textbf{entropy-production rate}
\(\sigma = \operatorname{tr}\!\big(D^{-1} Q P\,\Sigma\,P^{\mathsf T}Q^{\mathsf T}\big)\)
measures the system's irreversibility, and for the isotropic model it is
exactly \(\sigma = 2\,\omega^2\,\Pi/\gamma\) (with \(\Pi\) the
precision). It is strictly positive out of equilibrium and
\textbf{exactly zero if and only if \(\omega=0\)}. Entropy production is
the thermodynamic cost of staying far from equilibrium --- of keeping
the current running --- and it is the sharpest available statement of
the difference between the two regimes: not the shape of a path, but a
non-negative number that vanishes precisely at detailed balance. (Two
conventions, stated plainly: the choice \(D=\Gamma\) is the
fluctuation-- dissipation relation with \(k_{\mathrm B}T\equiv1\)
absorbed, so \(\sigma\) is a pure rate --- the dimensional
entropy-production rate is \(k_{\mathrm B}\sigma\) and the dissipated
power is \(k_{\mathrm B}T\sigma\); and crucially the nonequilibrium
character is certified by the \emph{current} and \(\sigma\),
\textbf{not} by the density --- the Gaussian \(\rho\) on its own looks
equilibrium-like, so it is the circulation, not the shape of the steady
state, that marks the regime as nonequilibrium.)

fig.~\ref{fig:steady} shows both panels over the same stationary
density. At equilibrium (a) the current field is identically zero and
\(\sigma=0\): nothing circulates and nothing is dissipated. In the NESS
(b) the current curls steadily around the mode and \(\sigma>0\): the
same density, now maintained by a perpetual, entropy-producing flow.
Read against the whole overview, this is the final turn of the one
thesis --- and the point at which the mathematics reconnects to the
motivation. A system that minimizes free energy need not merely come to
rest at its expected states; a \emph{nonequilibrium} steady state holds
those states while circulating through them, at a strictly positive
entropy cost. The overview opened by claiming that living systems occupy
exactly this regime; the model here does not prove that of any organism,
but it makes the regime itself --- the sustained current and its
irreversible price --- precise and measurable.

Two design choices keep the development honest and light. First, it
works over the integers \(\mathbb{Z}\) rather than the reals: the
results above are polynomial identities and sign facts whose truth does
not depend on the real-number completion, so \(\mathbb{Z}\) suffices and
the project needs no analysis library (it builds in about a second and
depends on \emph{nothing} but Lean core). The floating-point instances
live on the Python side; the two layers verify the same statements at
different resolutions.

Second --- and this is the crux for the free-energy bound --- the
logarithm's analytic properties are made \textbf{explicit hypotheses}
rather than hidden inside a library. Gibbs' inequality rests on exactly
two facts about \(\ln\): that it turns products into sums
(\(\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b\)), and the tangent-line bound \(\ln x \le x-1\).
But there is a subtlety that a green build alone will not catch, and the
development takes pains to get it right. Stating those two properties as
\emph{universal} hypotheses over \(\mathbb{Z}\) --- ``for \textbf{all}
positive integers'' --- would be a trap: no integer function can satisfy
the cleared tangent bound \(b-a \le b(L b - L a)\) at both orientations
of the pair \(\{2,3\}\) (integer rounding destroys the real-line
tangency), so such an antecedent has no model and every theorem beneath
it would be \textbf{vacuously} true. A proof assistant will happily
check a vacuous theorem: no \texttt{sorry}, clean axioms, and no
content.

The development therefore takes the log's properties only at the
\textbf{exact points each proof consumes} --- the tangent bound and
additivity at the specific belief/reference/evidence values --- which
the real \(\ln\) satisfies and which are jointly satisfiable over
\(\mathbb{Z}\). To \emph{prove} they are satisfiable rather than merely
assert it, \texttt{Sanity.lean} carries a matched pair of controls: a
negative one (\breaktt{relEntropy\_can\_be\_negative} --- a concrete
function for which relative entropy is \(-20\), so the tangent
hypothesis is genuinely necessary) and a \textbf{positive control} on a
\emph{genuine generative model}: \texttt{okL} is the 2-adic valuation
(an actually log-additive function), with belief \((4,2)\), posterior
\((2,4)\), evidence \(2\), and joint \((4,8) = (r_0 E, r_1 E)\) --- real
products, not hand-picked values. There every hypothesis provably holds
and the bound fires with a \emph{non-trivial} gap, \(-6 \le -4\), equal
to the relative entropy \(2\). Necessity and satisfiability together
certify the theorems are faithful, not vacuous.

The guarantee is auditable. The \texttt{Audit} module runs
\texttt{\#print\ axioms} on each headline theorem \emph{and each
witness}, and a build reports for every one that it
\texttt{depends\ on\ axioms:\ {[}propext,\ Quot.sound{]}} --- Lean's
standard trusted core --- and never \texttt{sorryAx}. The project's test
suite runs the real \texttt{lake\ build}, inspects that output, and
checks the non-vacuity witnesses are present, so the ``it is proved, and
it is not vacuous'' claim is itself tested rather than asserted. In the
spirit of the whole overview: the strongest thing you can say about a
mathematical claim is not that it looks right, or even that it computes
right, but that a machine has checked the proof --- and here, that you
can re-check the checker, and that what it checked has content.

\section{Planning: Policies as Action Sequences}\label{sec:planning}

sec.~\ref{sec:expected-free-energy} scored \emph{one} action: what is
the best thing to do right now? But the value of an action often lies in
what it makes possible \emph{next}. This section extends the same
currency to \textbf{policies} --- short sequences of actions --- and
shows that \emph{planning} is not a new rule bolted onto Active
Inference: it is expected free energy minimized over sequences. The core
is \breaktt{src/policy\_planning.py}; every value below is checked
against a hand-worked reference, and fig.~\ref{fig:planning} is
generated from the same live computation.

The agent of sec.~\ref{sec:expected-free-energy} returns, with the same
two actions --- a \textbf{cue} that reveals the hidden context and a
\textbf{bet} whose outcome model is state-independent --- and the same
\textbf{sharp} preferences under which the \emph{one-step} rule chose to
exploit. Now it plans over a horizon of \(T = 2\) steps. A policy
\(\pi = (a_1, \dots, a_T)\) induces a distribution over outcome
sequences; along each branch the belief evolves by exact Bayes rule, and
the plan's expected free energy is

\[
G(\pi) \;=\; -\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q(o_{1:T}\mid\pi)}\big[\ln p_{\text{pref}}(o_T)\big]}_{\text{pragmatic (terminal)}}
\;-\; \underbrace{I(s;\, o_{1:T} \mid \pi)}_{\text{epistemic (cumulative)}}.
\]

Two changes from the one-step \(G(a)\) are load-bearing. The
\textbf{pragmatic term is terminal}: payoffs are collected on the
\emph{final} outcome only, so an informative first action earns nothing
immediately --- its entire value is the sharper belief the final action
is then chosen under. And the \textbf{epistemic term is cumulative}: the
expected resolution of uncertainty over the whole sequence, which by the
chain rule of relative entropy is the mutual information
\(I(s; o_{1:T} \mid \pi)\) between the context and the \emph{entire}
outcome sequence (a fact the tests verify against an independently
computed mutual information).

The subtlety that makes this genuinely planning rather than bookkeeping
is \emph{when} the expectation is taken. Each branch is followed to its
end, the final action evaluated under that branch's posterior, and only
then averaged over branches. The nonlinearity of Bayes rule is preserved
inside the expectation --- which is exactly why information has value.
Averaging \emph{first} (the posterior-mixture shortcut) would collapse
every branch's posterior back to the prior and erase the value of
looking before leaping.

fig.~\ref{fig:planning} evaluates all four two-step policies. The winner
is \textbf{cue then bet}: look, resolve the uncertainty (epistemic value
\(+0.37\) nats), and \emph{then} place the wager the sharpened belief
licenses --- \(G = 0.07\). Betting first --- the one-step optimum under
these same preferences --- costs \(G = 0.43\): the terminal bet pays the
same either way, but it forgoes the information the plan could have
collected first. The explore/exploit balance of
sec.~\ref{sec:expected-free-energy} is revealed as a \emph{temporal}
structure: exploration is what exploitation looks like when it is
allowed to plan. One currency, one minimization, now over sequences ---
the agent defers gratification because \(\arg\min_\pi G(\pi)\) told it
to.

Branch enumeration is exact --- every one of \(O^T\) outcome sequences
is summed, no sampling, no RNG --- so the demonstration is deterministic
and reproducible. The horizon is deliberately short; tree search over
longer horizons, and approximate schemes for intractable ones, are the
natural next expansions, and are recorded as such in the roadmap.
