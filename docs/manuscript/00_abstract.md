# Abstract {#sec:abstract}

This tutorial connects Active Inference and synthetic intelligences to explicitly
specified informational and dynamical models. Finite categorical examples make
Bayesian conditioning, variational free energy, expected free energy, policy
planning, and observation-contingent commitments executable. A decision-value
example separates information gain in nats from improved expected utility and
observation cost. Gaussian examples distinguish deterministic relaxing paths
from stationary ensemble probability current, and marginal dependence from
conditional independence. A two-level model illustrates prediction and joint
conditioning without assuming a neural implementation. The standalone Python
package supplies reproducible figures, independent analytical checks, and
counterexamples; a separate Lean layer imports selected real-valued results
from a pinned revision of the broader FEP_Formal library. Generalized Notation
Notation and other Active Inference Institute resources provide further routes
for model specification and learning. The paper distinguishes computational
correctness, theorem hypotheses, thermodynamic units, and scientific
interpretation. These controlled demonstrations explain particular models;
they neither establish general intelligence nor infer life from circulation.
